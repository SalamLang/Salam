\chapter*{Closing Words}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Closing Words}
\markboth{Closing Words}{Closing Words}

This book began with one sentence: ``Hello, World!''. On that day there was
an empty editor and three lines of code. Today you know how a program keeps
values in memory, how it calculates and decides, how it repeats a task
without tiring, and how it divides a large job among small, named
functions. With arrays you have taken hold of collections of data, and with
strings of words and sentences; and in the last chapter you put all of this
together to build programs that are of some use.

More important than any tool is the way of looking at things that you have
learned: break a large problem into small steps, test each step on its own,
and when the program does not work, look at the error message as a guide,
not as a reproach.

\section*{This book stays beside you}

The three appendices at the end were made so that they would be useful after
you have finished reading. Whenever you are not sure whether a word is
reserved, look in appendix \ref{app:keywords}. Whenever your program gives a
message you do not understand, open appendix \ref{app:errors}. And whenever
you have written an exercise and want to weigh your solution against
another, the answers are in appendix \ref{app:solutions}. Remember that an
exercise has more than one right answer.

And if you want to write something right now, the Salam online editor is
always open:
\begin{center}
\url{https://editor.salamlang.ir}
\end{center}

\section*{The last program}

Every journey should be closed with a Salam program. Here is the last
program of the book, which is the first program with one more line:

\begin{salamcode}
func main:
    println "Hello, World!"
    println "See you soon, programmer!"
end
\end{salamcode}

\begin{salamout}
Hello, World!
See you soon, programmer!
\end{salamout}

Every programmer once began with this same ``Hello, World!''. You have begun
too; the rest of the road is yours.
