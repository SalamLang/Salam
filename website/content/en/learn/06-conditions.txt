<h2 id="if">if, else if, else</h2>
<p>The body of an <code>if</code> runs only when its condition holds. Test further conditions with <code>else if</code>, and cover everything that is left with <code>else</code>. The conditions are tried from the top, and only the first one that holds runs, so put the narrowest test first.</p>
{{example.conditions}}
<p>A score of 95 is also at least 70, but the program prints only <code>excellent</code>: once a branch has run, the rest are skipped.</p>

<h2 id="inline">Short conditions, and joining them</h2>
<p>A short condition fits on one line, with <code>end</code> at the end of the line. Join several conditions with <code>and</code> and <code>or</code>.</p>
{{example.cond-inline}}

<h2 id="words">Comparison words</h2>
<p>Every comparison operator also has a word: <code>eq</code> for <code>==</code>, <code>neq</code> for <code>!=</code>, <code>lt</code> and <code>gt</code> for <code>&lt;</code> and <code>&gt;</code>, and <code>lte</code> and <code>gte</code> for <code>&lt;=</code> and <code>&gt;=</code>. Use whichever reads better; they mean exactly the same. The example also shows the short form of <code>else if</code>: <code>else</code> followed directly by a condition.</p>
{{example.cond-words}}

<h2 id="logic">and, or and not</h2>
<p><code>and</code> and <code>or</code> stop as soon as the answer is known. If the left side of <code>and</code> is false, the right side is never evaluated, and the same goes for <code>or</code> when the left side is true. That makes the left side a guard for the right: test that a count is not zero before dividing by it, or that an index is in range before reading the array.</p>
{{example.cond-short}}
<p><code>not</code> binds tighter than a comparison. <code>not stock &gt; 5</code> would mean <code>(not stock) &gt; 5</code>, which is an error because <code>stock</code> is not a <code>bool</code>. Write <code>not (stock &gt; 5)</code>, or simply <code>stock &lt;= 5</code>. There is no <code>&amp;&amp;</code>, <code>||</code> or <code>!</code> in Salam; the compiler names the word to write instead.</p>

<h2 id="value">Choosing a value</h2>
<p>A variable declared inside a branch disappears at that branch's <code>end</code>. When each branch decides a value, declare the variable with <code>mut</code> before the <code>if</code> and assign it in the branches. For a choice between just two values, the ternary operator <code>condition ? a : b</code> is shorter, and both sides must have the same type.</p>
{{example.cond-scope}}

<h2 id="guard">Returning early</h2>
<p>Deeply nested ifs are hard to read. Inside a function, check for each problem in turn and <code>ret</code> as soon as one is found. What is left at the bottom is the normal case, with no nesting at all.</p>
{{example.cond-guard}}

<h2 id="float">Comparing floats</h2>
<p>Most decimal fractions cannot be stored exactly in binary, so a little rounding error creeps into arithmetic. <code>0.1 + 0.2</code> comes out as <code>0.30000000000000004</code>, and <code>==</code> against <code>0.3</code> is false. Compare floats by asking whether their difference is small enough. For money, keep amounts in whole units (cents, rials) as integers, or use the <a href="/std/decimal/">decimal</a> package.</p>
{{example.cond-float}}

<h2 id="rules">The rules a condition follows</h2>
<ul>
<li>A condition has to be a <code>bool</code>. <code>if number:</code> is an error; write <code>if number != 0:</code>.</li>
<li><code>if x = 3:</code> is an error that tells you to write <code>==</code> (or <code>eq</code>).</li>
<li><code>if x == 1 or 2:</code> is an error too, because <code>2</code> is not a <code>bool</code>. Write <code>x == 1 or x == 2</code>.</li>
<li>A condition that is always true, or always false, such as <code>if 2 &gt; 1:</code>, is rejected, because one of the branches would never run.</li>
<li>No branch may be empty. Instead of an empty branch, invert the condition with <code>not</code>.</li>
<li>A variable declared inside a branch cannot be used after the branch's <code>end</code>.</li>
</ul>
