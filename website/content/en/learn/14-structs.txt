<h2 id="define">Declaring a struct</h2>
<p>Every field has a name, a type and optionally a default value. Fields and methods are private by default; <code>pub</code> makes them reachable from outside the struct. Inside a method, <code>this</code> refers to the struct itself.</p>
{{example.structs}}

<h2 id="print">Creating and printing</h2>
<p>When you create an instance, any field you leave out takes its default value. A struct can be printed directly:</p>
{{example.struct-print}}

<h2 id="mut">Mutating methods</h2>
<p>A method that changes the struct's fields is declared <code>mut func</code>, and can only be called on a variable that is itself <code>mut</code>. No other method may change the struct.</p>
{{example.struct-mut}}

<h2 id="static">Static members</h2>
<p><code>static func</code> and <code>const</code> inside a struct belong to the type rather than to an instance; call them through the struct's name. They are a good home for ready-made instances.</p>
{{example.struct-static}}

<h2 id="embed">Composition with use</h2>
<p><code>use</code> places one struct inside another and makes its public fields and methods reachable directly: composition instead of inheritance.</p>
{{example.struct-embed}}
