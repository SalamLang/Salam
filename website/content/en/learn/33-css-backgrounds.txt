<h2 id="colors">Color and opacity</h2>
<p>A color is a value in one of a few notations, written inside the quotes of a property. A <em>name</em> such as <code>tomato</code> is the easiest to read and there are about 140 of them. A <em>hex code</em> such as <code>#0f766e</code> is red, green and blue as three pairs of digits, and a fourth pair adds transparency (<code>#0f766e80</code>). <code>rgb(37 99 235)</code> gives the same three channels as numbers from 0 to 255. <code>hsl(280 70% 40%)</code> gives a hue as an angle from 0 to 360, then saturation and lightness, which is the easiest notation to adjust by hand: keep the hue and make the lightness smaller to get a darker version. A slash adds an alpha channel to <code>rgb</code> and <code>hsl</code>, as in <code>rgb(15 118 110 / 40%)</code>. Two special values are <code>transparent</code> and <code>currentcolor</code>, which means the color of the text.</p>
<p><code>color</code> is the color of the text. It is inherited, so setting it on a container colors all the text inside. <code>background color</code> is the flat color behind the element. Always check that the two differ enough to read: body text needs a contrast ratio of at least 4.5 to 1 against its background, and a free contrast checker will tell you the number.</p>
{{example.web-bg-colors}}
<p>The two boxes at the bottom of the preview look alike but behave differently. <code>opacity</code> is a number from 0 to 1 and it fades the <em>whole element</em>, text and children included, so the bold text of the left box is faded too. A transparent color such as <code>rgb(15 118 110 / 40%)</code> fades only that one paint and leaves the text solid. Use opacity to fade a whole card in or out, and a transparent color when the text must stay readable.</p>
<div class="note"><p>A child can never be more opaque than its parent. If a parent has <code>opacity = 0.5</code>, no setting on the child brings it back to full strength. If you only want a pale background, use a transparent background color instead.</p></div>

<h2 id="background">The background properties</h2>
<p>An element can have a color and any number of images behind it, and each part has its own property. <code>background color</code> is the flat color at the bottom. <code>background image</code> is a list of images or gradients painted over it. <code>background size</code> sets how large an image is: a width and a height, a percentage, <code>cover</code> (fill the box and crop the rest) or <code>contain</code> (show everything and leave empty bands). <code>background position</code> places it, and <code>background position x</code> and <code>background position y</code> set one direction each. <code>background repeat</code> tiles it, with the values <code>repeat</code>, <code>no-repeat</code>, <code>repeat-x</code>, <code>repeat-y</code>, <code>space</code> and <code>round</code>.</p>
<p>Three more properties decide where the paint goes. <code>background origin</code> says which box the position is measured from, and <code>background clip</code> says how far the paint reaches: under the border, only under the padding, or only under the content. <code>background attachment</code> chooses what the image is attached to: <code>scroll</code> keeps it with the element, <code>fixed</code> keeps it still while the page moves, and <code>local</code> moves it with the element's own scrolling content. <code>background blend mode</code> mixes the layers with each other and is covered below.</p>
<p><code>background</code> is the shorthand for all of them. It takes the parts in any order, with a <code>/</code> between the position and the size:</p>
<pre class="code snip" dir="ltr"><code>box: height = 80 color = "white" background = "linear-gradient(#0ea5e9, #1d4ed8) center / cover no-repeat" content = "Shorthand" end</code></pre>
<div class="note"><p>The shorthand resets every part you leave out. If you write <code>background image</code> and then, a line later, <code>background = "#eee"</code>, the image is gone, because the shorthand sets the image to <code>none</code>. Use the shorthand first and the longhands after it, or use only longhands.</p></div>

<h2 id="layers">Several layers</h2>
<p>Most background properties take a comma separated list, one item per layer. The first layer in the list is on top. The example paints a layer of white dots over a gradient: the first item of each list belongs to the dots, the second to the gradient. Lists are matched by position, and a short list is repeated to fit. Only the last layer can hold a plain color, which is why the shorthand puts the color at the end.</p>
{{example.web-bg-layers}}
<p>The dots are an SVG image written straight into the page as a data URL, so there is no file to load. The characters that are special in a URL are written as percent codes: <code>%3C</code> for <code>&lt;</code>, <code>%3E</code> for <code>&gt;</code>, <code>%23</code> for <code>#</code> and <code>%27</code> for a single quote. Keep such a long string in a constant above the <code>page:</code> block and use the constant's name in the property.</p>
<p>The next example shows <code>background clip</code> and <code>background origin</code> on boxes with a dashed border. The three boxes at the left paint the same color under the border, only under the padding and only under the content. The last one has a gradient that starts at the outer edge of the border and not at the padding.</p>
{{example.web-bg-clip}}

<h2 id="gradients">Gradients</h2>
<p>A gradient is an image made by the browser, so it goes wherever an image is accepted: <code>background image</code>, <code>mask image</code>, <code>border image source</code> and <code>list style image</code>. <code>linear-gradient</code> blends colors along a line: give an angle such as <code>90deg</code> (a quarter turn, left to right) or a direction such as <code>to right</code>, then the colors. <code>radial-gradient</code> spreads from a point outward in a circle or an ellipse, and <code>circle at 30% 30%</code> moves its center. <code>conic-gradient</code> sweeps around a point like a clock hand and is the way to draw pie charts and color wheels. The <code>repeating-</code> versions repeat the pattern forever.</p>
<p>A color stop can have a position. Two stops at the same position make a hard edge instead of a blend, which is how the "hard stop" box draws two flat halves, and a stop written as <code>#0f766e 0 10px</code> covers a range, which is how the stripes are made.</p>
{{example.web-bg-gradients}}
<p>A gradient can also fill letters. Give the element a gradient background, cut the background to the shape of the text with <code>background clip = "text"</code>, and make the text color transparent so the background shows through.</p>
{{example.web-bg-text}}

<h2 id="images">Fitting pictures into boxes</h2>
<p>An <code>image</code> element has the size you give it with <code>width</code> and <code>height</code>, and the picture inside rarely has the same shape. <code>object fit</code> decides what happens. <code>fill</code> stretches the picture to the box and distorts it. <code>contain</code> shows the whole picture and leaves bands. <code>cover</code> fills the box and crops what does not fit, which is what you want for photos in a grid of cards. <code>none</code> keeps the natural size, and <code>scale-down</code> picks the smaller of <code>none</code> and <code>contain</code>. When a picture is cropped, <code>object position</code> chooses which part stays, with the same values as <code>background position</code>. The last picture in the example keeps its right side.</p>
{{example.web-bg-fit}}
<p>The pictures in these examples are small PNG and SVG images inside the page, because a page can use a <code>data:image/...</code> address anywhere an image address is accepted. Other kinds of <code>data:</code> address are refused by the compiler with an error, since they could run code.</p>
<p><code>image rendering</code> chooses how a picture is resampled when it is scaled. The default is smooth. <code>pixelated</code> keeps hard square pixels, which is right for pixel art and QR codes. <code>image orientation</code> decides whether a photo is turned by the rotation its camera recorded: <code>from-image</code> does, and it is the default in current browsers, while <code>none</code> shows the pixels as stored. The newer <code>object view box</code> crops and zooms the visible part of a picture without changing its box, but it is not supported by every browser yet.</p>
{{example.web-bg-pixels}}

<h2 id="filters">Filters</h2>
<p><code>filter</code> applies a graphic effect to an element and everything inside it. Each effect is a function: <code>blur(2px)</code>, <code>grayscale(1)</code>, <code>sepia(0.9)</code>, <code>hue-rotate(120deg)</code>, <code>brightness(1.3)</code>, <code>contrast(1.4)</code>, <code>saturate(3)</code>, <code>invert(1)</code>, <code>opacity(0.5)</code> and <code>drop-shadow(6px 6px 4px black)</code>. Write several with spaces between them and they are applied from left to right, so the order changes the result. The preview shows the same picture eight times. The component at the top of the file, <code>Tile</code>, takes a label and a filter, so each tile is one short line.</p>
{{example.web-bg-filter}}
<p>The values of a filter can change while the page runs. The next example reads two sliders and writes a new <code>filter</code> value to the box each time one of them moves. Try both sliders at once.</p>
{{example.web-bg-live}}
<p><code>backdrop filter</code> takes the same functions but applies them to what is <em>behind</em> the element, seen through its transparent parts. Together with a semi-transparent background it makes frosted glass. The card needs a background that is not fully opaque, otherwise there is nothing to see through.</p>
{{example.web-bg-glass}}
<div class="note"><p>Blur is expensive for the browser, especially a large one on a large area. Use it on small elements, and keep an eye on scrolling speed on phones. <code>drop-shadow</code> follows the real shape of the content including transparent pixels, while <code>box shadow</code> (next lesson) follows the box.</p></div>

<h2 id="blend">Blend modes and isolation</h2>
<p>Normally a box covers what is behind it. A blend mode mixes the two colors instead. <code>mix blend mode</code> works on the element itself and what is behind it, and <code>background blend mode</code> works between the background layers of one element. <code>multiply</code> always gives a darker result, as if two transparent colored films were stacked, and <code>screen</code> gives a lighter one. <code>overlay</code>, <code>difference</code>, <code>color-dodge</code> and the other modes are worth trying, because the formulas are easier to see than to read.</p>
<p>By default a blend reaches everything behind the element, all the way to the page. <code>isolation = isolate</code> turns an element into a group of its own, so the blending inside it stays inside. The left example is isolated: the two circles multiply with each other and with the white card, and nothing else on the page changes.</p>
{{example.web-bg-blend}}

<h2 id="shapes">Clips, masks and shaped text</h2>
<p><code>clip path</code> cuts an element to a shape. Everything outside the shape is not drawn and also stops receiving clicks. The shapes are <code>circle(50%)</code>, <code>ellipse(50% 40% at 50% 50%)</code>, <code>inset(10% 0 10% 0 round 20px)</code> and <code>polygon(...)</code>, a list of points as x and y pairs, where the star in the example has ten points. Because the cut is exact, anything drawn outside the box, such as a shadow, is cut too.</p>
{{example.web-bg-clippath}}
<p><code>mask</code> does a similar job with soft edges. Its image decides how visible each point of the element is. Where the mask is opaque the element shows, and where it is transparent the element is hidden, so a gradient from black to transparent is a fade out. <code>mask image</code>, <code>mask size</code>, <code>mask position</code>, <code>mask repeat</code>, <code>mask origin</code> and <code>mask clip</code> work like their background cousins. <code>mask mode</code> chooses whether transparency (<code>alpha</code>) or brightness (<code>luminance</code>) counts, <code>mask composite</code> says how several mask layers combine, and <code>mask type</code> is only for masks drawn with the SVG <code>mask</code> element.</p>
{{example.web-bg-mask}}
<p>The last shape tool changes the text and not the box. A <code>float</code> normally makes text wrap around its rectangle. <code>shape outside</code> makes the text follow another shape, such as <code>circle(50%)</code>, so the lines tuck in next to the curve. <code>shape margin</code> adds room between the shape and the words. With a picture or gradient as the shape, <code>shape image threshold</code> says how opaque a pixel must be to count. The shape changes only the wrapping, so the box still needs its own <code>border radius</code> or <code>clip path</code> to look round.</p>
{{example.web-bg-shape}}

<h2 id="forms">Colors for form controls</h2>
<p>Browsers draw checkboxes, radio buttons, sliders and progress bars themselves. <code>accent color</code> recolors the active part of all of them with one property and keeps them native, so they stay accessible. <code>caret color</code> colors the blinking cursor inside a text field. The checkbox in the example also reacts to a click: its handler asks the browser for the box's <code>checked</code> property and writes the answer into a paragraph.</p>
{{example.web-bg-forms}}
<div class="note"><p>Do not use color as the only way to say something. Red text for an error is invisible to a person who cannot tell red from green, so add a word or an icon as well. In a page that supports dark mode, test every color pair in both themes.</p></div>
