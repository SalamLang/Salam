pkg.intro <<
<p>This package is the middle of everything to do with sound in Salam. A <code>Clip</code> holds the sound itself: plain 32 bit floating point samples between -1 and 1, one frame after another, with the channels side by side. Reading and writing files is the job of the format packages, <a href="/std/audio/wav/">audio/wav</a> today, and <a href="/std/audio/codec/">audio/codec</a> opens any of them without being told which. Filters, a Fourier transform and careful resampling live in <a href="/std/audio/dsp/">audio/dsp</a>.</p>
<p>What is here is the clip and everything that does not need to know a file format: cutting, joining, mixing, fading, measuring how loud something is, moving between mono and stereo and between sample rates, and the most significant bit first bitstream reader that every audio codec is written on top of.</p>
<h3>Things to know</h3>
<ul>
<li>Samples are 32 bit floats. Every clip owns that memory; release it with <code>Free</code>.</li>
<li>Because a float carries 24 bits of mantissa, 8, 16 and 24 bit sound makes the round trip through a clip <strong>exactly</strong>. Only a true 32 bit integer source loses its lowest 8 bits.</li>
<li>A three minute stereo song at 44100 is about 63 MB as a clip, so decode a long file in pieces rather than all at once.</li>
<li><code>bits</code> remembers the depth a clip was read at, so writing it again keeps that depth. A clip made with <code>New</code> has no depth, and an encoder then picks 16 bit.</li>
<li>Anything that fails returns an empty clip rather than stopping the program, so check with <code>IsEmpty</code>.</li>
<li><code>MsbReader</code> reads the most significant bit first. The <code>BitReader</code> in <a href="/std/compress/">compress</a> reads the other way round, for DEFLATE, and is not interchangeable with it.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="guide-clip">A clip of sound</h2>
<p>A clip is a rate, a channel count, a frame count and the samples. <code>New</code> gives you silence to fill in; <code>SetSample</code> and <code>Sample</code> reach one sample at a time by frame and channel. A frame is one sample for every channel, so a stereo clip of 100 frames holds 200 samples.</p>
<p>Every clip holds memory of its own, so release each one with <code>Free</code>. Reading or writing outside the clip is not an error: <code>Sample</code> gives 0 and <code>SetSample</code> does nothing, which keeps loops at the edges simple.</p>

<h2 id="guide-measure">How loud is it</h2>
<p><code>Peak</code> is the furthest any single sample gets from zero and <code>RMS</code> follows loudness as an ear hears it. Both have a decibel form, where 0 dB is as loud as a sample can be and everything quieter is negative. <code>GainToDb</code> and <code>DbToGain</code> move between a plain multiplier and decibels.</p>
<p>Silence has no decibel value, so <code>GainToDb(0.0)</code> returns <code>SilenceFloorDb</code> instead of running off to minus infinity. <code>FirstSound</code> and <code>LastSound</code> find where the sound really starts and stops, which is what <code>TrimSilence</code> is built from.</p>

<h2 id="guide-edit">Cutting and joining</h2>
<p><code>Slice</code> and <code>Trim</code> take a piece out, by frame or by time; <code>Concat</code> puts one clip after another and <code>Mix</code> lays them on top of each other. <code>Normalize</code> scales a clip so its peak lands exactly where you ask, and the two fades shape the ends so a cut does not click.</p>
<p>Joining and mixing need both clips to agree on the sample rate and the channel count, and return an empty clip when they do not. Use <code>ToRate</code> and <code>ToChannels</code> first to bring them into line.</p>

<h2 id="guide-convert">Rates, channels and depths</h2>
<p><code>ToMono</code> averages the channels together and <code>ToStereo</code> spreads one channel across two. <code>ToRate</code> changes the sample rate by interpolating between samples, which is quick and good enough for speech; for music use <code>ResampleSinc</code> from <a href="/std/audio/dsp/">audio/dsp</a>. <code>ToBits</code> does not write a file, it rounds the samples to the steps of a depth so you can hear what that depth will cost.</p>
<p><code>Deinterleave</code> hands back one clip per channel, each of which you must free, and <code>Interleave</code> weaves them back together.</p>

<h2 id="guide-formats">Which format is this</h2>
<p><code>DetectFormat</code> looks at the first few bytes and <code>FormatFromExt</code> at the file name. Both answer with a <code>Format</code> number, which <code>FormatName</code> turns into a short name. Knowing the format does not mean this build can decode it: ask <code>CanDecode</code> in <a href="/std/audio/codec/">audio/codec</a> for that.</p>
<p>An <code>Info</code> is what a file says about itself, which the format packages fill in by reading a header rather than the whole file. It is the cheap way to show a length or a bit rate in a listing.</p>

<h2 id="guide-msb">Reading a bitstream</h2>
<p>Compressed audio is not laid out in bytes, it is laid out in bits, and every format from MP3 to FLAC counts those bits from the most significant end. <code>MsbReader</code> does that, with <code>MsbReadSigned</code> for two's complement fields and <code>MsbUnary</code> for the run of zeros that Rice and Golomb codes are built from. <code>MsbWriter</code> is the same thing in reverse.</p>
<p>A read past the end does not stop the program: it hands back zeros and sets <code>bad</code>, which stays set, so one check after a group of reads is enough. Note again that <code>compress</code> has a bit reader of its own that runs the other way round, for DEFLATE; do not use it for audio.</p>
