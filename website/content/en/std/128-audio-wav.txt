pkg.intro <<
<p>WAVE is the format to reach for when nothing should be lost. It is a RIFF container holding a <code>fmt </code> chunk that says what the samples are and a <code>data</code> chunk holding them, and this package reads and writes it without any outside library.</p>
<p>Integer samples at 8, 16, 24 and 32 bits are all handled, as are IEEE floating point samples and the two eight bit companding schemes from telephony, A-law and mu-law. <code>OpenWAV</code> and <code>SaveWAV</code> are the short way in; <a href="/std/audio-codec/">audio/codec</a> is shorter still when you do not want to name the format at all.</p>
<h3>Things to know</h3>
<ul>
<li>8, 16 and 24 bit PCM makes the round trip through a <a href="/std/audio/">clip</a> exactly. A true 32 bit integer file loses its lowest 8 bits, because a clip holds 32 bit floats; write such a file with <code>EncodeWAVFloat</code> instead, which keeps every bit.</li>
<li><code>EncodeWAV</code> writes at the depth the clip remembers in <code>bits</code>, and 16 bit when the clip has none. <code>EncodeWAVBits</code> takes the depth from you.</li>
<li>A companded file is a quarter the size of 24 bit PCM and does lose a little, most of it where the sound is loud. Near silence the error is smaller than one step of 16 bit PCM.</li>
<li><code>ProbeWAV</code> reads the header alone, so a listing of a thousand files does not have to decode any of them.</li>
<li><code>Chunks</code> hands back a vector you must free. <code>FindChunk</code> allocates nothing, and is what the decoder uses.</li>
</ul>
>>
