pkg.intro <<
<p>This is the arithmetic behind sound: a Fourier transform to see what frequencies are in a block, windows to taper the block first, filters to shape it and resampling to move it between rates. The codecs in this family lean on the same code, which is why it lives here rather than inside any one of them.</p>
<p>Everything works on a <a href="/std/audio/">clip</a> or on plain arrays of <code>f64</code>. The transform is radix-2, so a length has to be a power of two: use <code>NextPow2</code> to pad up to one.</p>
<h3>Things to know</h3>
<ul>
<li><code>FFT</code> and <code>IFFT</code> work in place on two arrays, the real parts and the imaginary parts. <code>IFFT</code> already divides by the length, so it undoes <code>FFT</code> exactly.</li>
<li>A real signal shows the same strength at bin <em>k</em> and at bin <em>n - k</em>, so only the first half of a spectrum tells you anything new.</li>
<li>Taper a block with <code>ApplyWindow</code> before transforming it, or a tone that does not fit a whole number of cycles will smear across every bin.</li>
<li>The filters follow the well known cookbook formulas. A <code>Biquad</code> remembers two samples, so <code>BiquadReset</code> it before a different clip, and prefer <code>BiquadProcessAll</code>, which gives each channel its own copy.</li>
<li><code>BiquadResponse</code> tells you what a filter does at a frequency without running any sound through it, which makes filters easy to test.</li>
<li><code>ResampleSinc</code> is the one to use on music; <code>ToRate</code> in <a href="/std/audio/">audio</a> is the quick linear version.</li>
</ul>
>>

doc.KaiserBeta = The beta the resampler uses for its Kaiser window.
doc.QualityCubic = A curve through four samples. A good middle.
doc.QualityLinear = Straight lines between samples. Quick, and rough on music.
doc.QualitySinc = A windowed sinc over many samples. The one to use on music.
doc.WindowBlackman = Leaks less than Hann at the cost of a wider peak.
doc.WindowBlackmanHarris = Leaks very little, for when a quiet tone sits next to a loud one.
doc.WindowHamming = Like Hann but it does not quite reach zero, which trades a little leaking for a narrower peak.
doc.WindowHann = The usual first choice: a raised cosine that reaches zero at both ends.
doc.WindowKaiser = A window you can tune with beta: higher beta leaks less and widens the peak.
doc.WindowRectangular = No window at all. Sharpest in frequency, worst for leaking.
doc.WindowSine = Half a sine. Used where two windows have to add up to one.
doc.WindowVorbis = The window the Vorbis format uses, which also adds up to one when it overlaps itself.
doc.AllPass = A filter that passes everything at full strength but shifts the phase.
doc.ApplyWindow = Multiplies an array by a window in place, which is what you do before a transform.
doc.BandPass = A filter that passes a band around the frequency and nothing else.
doc.BesselI0 = The modified Bessel function of the first kind, order zero, which the Kaiser window is built from.
doc.BinFrequency = Which frequency a bin stands for, given the length and the sample rate.
doc.BiquadProcess = Runs the filter over one channel of a clip, in place.
doc.BiquadProcessAll = Runs a fresh copy of the filter over every channel, so the channels do not bleed into one another.
doc.BiquadReset = Forgets the two remembered samples. Do this before filtering a different clip.
doc.BiquadResponse = How much the filter passes at a frequency, as a plain multiplier. Useful for checking a filter without any sound.
doc.BiquadStep = Pushes one sample through the filter and returns the result.
doc.CAbs = How far the number is from zero.
doc.CAdd = Two complex numbers added.
doc.CArg = The angle of the number, in radians.
doc.CConj = The conjugate, the same number with the imaginary part negated.
doc.CFromPolar = A complex number built from a size and an angle.
doc.CMul = Two complex numbers multiplied.
doc.CScale = A complex number times a plain number.
doc.CSub = One complex number taken from another.
doc.Chorus = A new clip thickened by a copy whose delay wanders, which sounds like more than one player.
doc.CompressorProcess = Runs a compressor over a clip, in place.
doc.Correlation = How alike two clips are, from 1 for the same to 0 for unrelated. Handy for checking that a round trip kept the sound.
doc.DCRemove = Shifts the clip so its average is zero.
doc.Delay = Mixes a delayed copy back in, in place.
doc.DetectBPM = The tempo in beats per minute, by scoring how well each candidate lines up with the onsets.
doc.DetectOnsets = The frames where something starts, found from spectral flux.
doc.DetectPitch = The pitch in hertz at a position, by autocorrelation. It takes the first strong peak rather than the biggest, which is what keeps it from answering an octave too low.
doc.Echo = A new clip with repeats trailing off behind it, longer than the one you gave it.
doc.FFT = The Fourier transform in place over two arrays, the real parts and the imaginary parts. The length must be a power of two.
doc.FFTVec = The Fourier transform over two vectors, which saves you reaching for pointers.
doc.Flanger = A new clip with a short wandering delay mixed in, the sweeping sound of a jet.
doc.FreeSpectrum = Releases the magnitudes a spectrogram holds.
doc.GateProcess = Silences a clip wherever it sits below a threshold, which is how a noise gate works.
doc.HighPass = A filter that passes what is above the frequency.
doc.HighShelf = Lifts or cuts everything above the frequency.
doc.IFFT = The inverse transform in place, already divided by the length so that it undoes FFT exactly.
doc.IFFTVec = The inverse transform over two vectors.
doc.IsPow2 = True when the number is a power of two, which is what FFT needs.
doc.LimiterProcess = Holds a clip under a ceiling. Unlike a compressor it guarantees the ceiling, by clamping what the fast attack still let past.
doc.LoudnessLUFS = Loudness the way broadcasters measure it, after the ITU-R BS.1770 weighting.
doc.LowPass = A filter that passes what is below the frequency. Q of 0.707 is flat with no bump.
doc.LowShelf = Lifts or cuts everything below the frequency.
doc.Magnitude = How strong each bin is, which is what a spectrum usually means.
doc.MakeWindow = A whole window as a vector.
doc.NextPow2 = The first power of two at least this big, for padding a window out to a length FFT accepts.
doc.NormalizeLUFS = Scales a clip so its loudness lands on a target in LUFS.
doc.Notch = A filter that removes a narrow band and passes the rest, for killing a hum.
doc.PeakBin = Which bin is strongest, or -1 when there are none. Ties go to the lower bin.
doc.Peaking = Lifts or cuts a band around the frequency by so many decibels.
doc.Phase = The angle of each bin.
doc.PitchShift = A new clip the same length but a different pitch, by stretching and then resampling.
doc.PowerSpectrum = The square of each bin's strength, which adds up like energy does.
doc.ResampleCubic = Changes the sample rate with a Catmull-Rom curve.
doc.ResampleSinc = Changes the sample rate with a windowed sinc, filtering out what the new rate cannot hold.
doc.ResampleTo = Changes the sample rate, picking the method from a Quality number.
doc.Reverb = A new clip with a room around it, built from four comb delays.
doc.STFT = A spectrogram of a clip: a window slid along it, each position transformed. Release it with FreeSpectrum.
doc.SpectralCentroid = Where the weight of a row sits, in hertz. It tracks how bright a sound is.
doc.SpectralFlatness = How noise-like a row is, from 0 for a pure tone to 1 for white noise.
doc.SpectralFlux = How much a row grew over the one before it, which is what onset detection is built on.
doc.SpectralRolloff = The frequency below which a share of the energy sits.
doc.SpectrumAt = One magnitude, by row and bin. Outside the spectrogram it is 0.
doc.TimeStretch = A new clip the same pitch but a different length, by overlapping and adding windows.
doc.Tremolo = Moves the level up and down in place, leaving the pitch alone.
doc.TruePeakDb = The peak after oversampling, which catches peaks that fall between two samples.
doc.WindowName = The short name of a window.
doc.WindowSum = Every point of a window added up, for undoing the gain a window costs you.
doc.WindowValue = One point of a window. The parameter is the Kaiser beta and is ignored by the others.
doc.ZeroCrossingRate = How often the signal crosses zero per sample. It rises with pitch and with noise.
doc.Biquad = A two pole, two zero filter: five coefficients and the two samples it remembers.
doc.Biquad.b0 = The first feed forward coefficient.
doc.Biquad.b1 = The second feed forward coefficient.
doc.Biquad.b2 = The third feed forward coefficient.
doc.Biquad.a1 = The first feedback coefficient, already divided through by a0.
doc.Biquad.a2 = The second feedback coefficient, already divided through by a0.
doc.Biquad.z1 = The first remembered sample. Reset it before filtering a new clip.
doc.Biquad.z2 = The second remembered sample.
doc.Complex = A complex number, used by the Fourier transform.
doc.Complex.re = The real part.
doc.Complex.im = The imaginary part.
doc.Compressor = How hard to squeeze a signal and how fast to react.
doc.Compressor.threshold = The level in decibels above which the squeezing starts.
doc.Compressor.ratio = How much of the excess to keep. A ratio of 4 lets a quarter of it through.
doc.Compressor.attack = How many seconds to take hold once the signal goes over.
doc.Compressor.release = How many seconds to let go once it comes back under.
doc.Compressor.makeup = Decibels of gain added afterwards, to win back what the squeezing cost.
doc.Spectrum = A spectrogram: one magnitude row per analysis frame.
doc.Spectrum.bins = How many frequency bins each row holds, which is half the window.
doc.Spectrum.frames = How many rows there are.
doc.Spectrum.rate = The sample rate the clip had, needed to turn a bin into a frequency.
doc.Spectrum.hop = How many samples apart the rows are.
doc.Spectrum.data = The magnitudes, row after row. Release them with FreeSpectrum.
