pkg.intro <<
<p>This package is the arithmetic of music rather than of sound: what a note is, which notes belong together, and what frequency each one wants. It makes no sound itself, which <a href="/std/audio/synth/">audio/synth</a> does.</p>
<p>Notes are MIDI numbers, so 60 is middle C and 69 is the A tuned to 440. <code>NoteToFreq</code> turns one into a frequency in equal temperament, and <code>JustFreq</code> and <code>PythagoreanRatio</code> are there when equal temperament is not what you want.</p>
<h3>Things to know</h3>
<ul>
<li>Scales and chords come back as vectors of semitone steps, which the caller frees.</li>
<li><code>NoteName</code> returns a string the caller frees. <code>ParseNote</code> reads one back and accepts flats and either case, so it round-trips its own output.</li>
<li><strong>Persian music does not fit twelve semitones.</strong> A dastgah is therefore given in cents, not semitone steps: the third of Shur sits at 350 cents, between a minor and a major third, where no piano key is. <code>NoteToFreqCents</code> is how you ask for it, and <code>KoronCents</code> and <code>SoriCents</code> are the accidentals that get you there.</li>
<li>The seven dastgah here are the frequencies of their tonic rows. A real performance moves between gusheh within a dastgah, which is a matter of melody rather than of tuning.</li>
</ul>
>>
