pkg.intro <<
<p><code>cron</code> parses the usual five-field cron expression (or six, with a leading seconds field), matches it against a time, computes the next matching moment, and runs registered jobs on a one-second tick. It is a library, not a service: it runs inside your program, uses your program's threads and stops when your program stops.</p>
<p>The five fields are minute, hour, day of month, month and day of week, in that order. Each field accepts <code>*</code>, a number, a range <code>a-b</code>, a list <code>a,b,c</code>, and a step <code>*/n</code> or <code>a-b/n</code>. Months and weekdays also accept three-letter English names such as <code>jan</code> and <code>mon</code>. As in Vixie cron, when both day fields are restricted a time matches either of them; when one is <code>*</code>, both must match.</p>
<pre class="code"><code>*/15 * * * *        every quarter hour
0 9-17 * * mon-fri  every hour from 9 to 17 on weekdays
30 2 1 * *          at 02:30 on the first day of every month
0 0 29 feb *        on February 29</code></pre>
<p>The macros <code>@yearly</code>, <code>@annually</code>, <code>@monthly</code>, <code>@weekly</code>, <code>@daily</code>, <code>@midnight</code> and <code>@hourly</code> expand to the usual five fields.</p>

<h2 id="guide-parse">Parsing and the next run</h2>
<p><code>Parse</code> returns an <code>Expr</code> with <code>ok</code> false when the text is not a valid expression. Check <code>ok</code> before you use it. <code>Next</code> returns the first minute strictly after the given time that matches, in the given time zone, or 0 when it finds none within the scan limit.</p>
{{example.cron-quickstart}}
<p>Times are UTC seconds since the epoch. <code>Next</code> asks the zone database for the offset at each candidate minute, so daylight saving changes are handled. When the zone does not exist, every call takes <code>fallback_minutes</code> instead, which is also what the tests use to stay independent of the machine.</p>

<h2 id="guide-match">Matching a time you already have</h2>
<p><code>MatchFields</code> takes the local minute, hour, day, month and weekday directly, so you can use it on values from your own clock or from a database. <code>MatchesLocal</code> takes a wall-clock instant in seconds, already shifted by the zone offset, and is what the scheduler uses internally.</p>
{{example.cron-match}}
<p>Weekdays are 0 for Sunday through 6 for Saturday; 7 is accepted and treated as Sunday. <code>MatchFields</code> returns false for an expression whose <code>ok</code> is false.</p>

<h2 id="guide-zone">Zones and offsets</h2>
<p>The scheduler keeps one zone for all jobs. <code>SetZone</code> sets its name and the fallback offset in minutes, used when the name cannot be loaded. <code>OffsetSeconds</code> returns the offset of any zone at an instant, which is useful outside the scheduler too.</p>
<pre class="code"><code>cron.SetZone("Asia/Tehran", 210)
off := cron.OffsetSeconds("Asia/Tehran", 210, 1700000000 as i64)
println off / (60 as i64)</code></pre>
<p>The default zone is <code>UTC</code> with a fallback of 0. Call <code>SetZone</code> before <code>Run</code> or <code>RunBackground</code>; changing it later affects the next minute, not a job that is already running.</p>

<h2 id="guide-jobs">Jobs and the scheduler</h2>
<p><code>Add</code> registers a function under a name and an expression and returns the job id, or -1 when the expression does not parse. A job handler takes the job id as an <code>i64</code>. Register every job before you start the scheduler.</p>
{{example.cron-schedule}}
<p><code>Run</code> blocks and fires the jobs that are due once a minute; <code>RunBackground</code> runs the same loop on its own thread and returns at once. <code>RunDue</code> fires what is due at the given time without waiting for the next minute, which is what a test or a manual tick uses. In every form a job that is still running from an earlier firing is skipped, not queued, so a slow job cannot pile up.</p>
<p><code>Count</code>, <code>Name</code> and <code>IsBusy</code> inspect the table. <code>Stop</code> asks the loop to end after the current job; it does not wait for a running job, and it can be called from a signal handler or another thread.</p>
<pre class="code"><code>cron.SetZone("UTC", 0)
cron.Add("cleanup", "0 3 * * *", cleanup)
cron.RunBackground()
time.Sleep(60000)
cron.Stop()</code></pre>

<h2 id="guide-seconds">Seconds</h2>
<p>A sixth field in front of the minute gives second resolution: <code>*/10 * * * * *</code> fires every ten seconds and <code>0 30 9 * * mon</code> at 09:30:00 on Mondays. With five fields nothing changes: the job fires once in the matching minute.</p>

<h2 id="guide-readable">Setting a time without cron syntax</h2>
<p>Most schedules are a clock time, so they have helpers that take <code>"HH:MM"</code> or <code>"HH:MM:SS"</code>.</p>
<pre class="code"><code>cron.DailyAt("report", "10:30", report)
cron.WeeklyAt("backup", "mon-fri", "02:00", backup)
cron.MonthlyAt("invoice", 1, "08:00", invoice)
cron.Every("heartbeat", 30, beat)
cron.EveryBetween("poll", 20, 40, poll)
cron.Once("launch", 1792900000 as i64, launch)</code></pre>
<p>All of them return the job id, or -1 when the time does not parse. <code>Every</code> counts from now; <code>EveryBetween</code> picks a random gap between its two bounds each time, which spreads load.</p>
{{example.cron-jobs}}

<h2 id="guide-control">Controlling jobs</h2>
<p>Ids are never reused. <code>Remove</code> and <code>RemoveByTag</code> retire jobs, <code>Find</code> looks one up by name, and <code>SetTags</code> takes a comma-separated list. <code>Limit</code> stops a job after n runs, <code>StartAt</code> and <code>StopAt</code> bound it in time, <code>FromCompletion</code> counts an interval from the end of a run, and <code>Queue</code> makes a firing that finds the job busy wait instead of being skipped. <code>RunNow</code> runs a job immediately, <code>NextRun</code> reports its next firing, and <code>Runs</code>, <code>LastRun</code> and <code>LastFinished</code> report what happened. <code>OnBefore</code> and <code>OnAfter</code> register one handler each that runs around every job. <code>StopWait</code> stops the loop and waits for running jobs up to a timeout in milliseconds.</p>

<h2 id="guide-gocron">Compared with gocron</h2>
<p>Salam cron covers gocron's schedule kinds and job options, except distribution.</p>
<table>
<tr><th>gocron</th><th>Salam cron</th></tr>
<tr><td>CronJob(expr, withSeconds)</td><td>Add with five or six fields</td></tr>
<tr><td>DurationJob, DurationRandomJob</td><td>Every, EveryBetween</td></tr>
<tr><td>DailyJob, WeeklyJob, MonthlyJob</td><td>DailyAt, WeeklyAt, MonthlyAt (interval of 1)</td></tr>
<tr><td>OneTimeJob</td><td>Once</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithName, WithTags</td><td>the name argument, SetTags</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithLimitedRuns</td><td>Limit</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithStartAt, WithStopAt</td><td>StartAt, StopAt</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithIntervalFromCompletion</td><td>FromCompletion</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithSingletonMode (reschedule or wait)</td><td>skip by default, Queue to wait</td></tr>
<tr><td>Before and After listeners</td><td>OnBefore, OnAfter (error and panic listeners: none)</td></tr>
<tr><td>NextRun, LastRun, RunNow, RemoveJob</td><td>NextRun, LastRun, RunNow, Remove</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithLocation</td><td>SetZone (one zone for all jobs)</td></tr>
<tr><td>WithStopTimeout, Shutdown</td><td>StopWait</td></tr>
<tr><td>Distributed elector and locker, monitors, concurrency limit, daily or weekly every n</td><td>not available</td></tr>
</table>
<p>For a readable clock time, <code>DailyAt("report", "10:30", f)</code> is shorter than gocron's <code>DailyJob(1, NewAtTimes(NewAtTime(10, 30, 0)))</code>, and a plain cron string works in both.</p>

<h2 id="guide-limits">What to know</h2>
<ul>
<li>Resolution is one second, and only for jobs with a six-field expression or an interval. Five-field jobs fire once in their minute.</li>
<li>Jobs run on their own threads and share the process with you. A panic, a blocking read or a long loop in one job affects the whole program.</li>
<li><code>Stop</code> is cooperative: the loop checks the flag several times a second, so a job that ignores its own cancellation can keep running.</li>
<li>The package does not persist jobs. If you need them after a restart, store the name and expression yourself and register them again at startup.</li>
<li>A day-of-month and day-of-week pair that are both restricted matches either field, as in Vixie cron. This is often surprising; prefer a single day field when you can.</li>
</ul>

doc.Expr = A parsed schedule: five bit masks, two flags that say which day field was a star, and an <code>ok</code> flag.
doc.Expr.minutes = The minutes of the hour that match, as a bit mask.
doc.Expr.hours = The hours of the day that match, as a bit mask.
doc.Expr.days = The days of the month that match, as a bit mask.
doc.Expr.months = The months that match, as a bit mask.
doc.Expr.weekdays = The weekdays that match, as a bit mask; Sunday is bit 0.
doc.Expr.dom_star = True when the day-of-month field was <code>*</code>.
doc.Expr.dow_star = True when the day-of-week field was <code>*</code>.
doc.Expr.seconds = The seconds that match, as a bit mask; only used when <code>with_seconds</code> is true.
doc.Expr.with_seconds = True when the text had a leading seconds field.
doc.Expr.ok = True when the text parsed; every other function refuses an expression whose <code>ok</code> is false.
doc.Parse = Parses five fields, or six with a leading seconds field, or a macro such as <code>@daily</code>. Returns an expression with <code>ok</code> false on a bad field count, a number out of range, a broken range, a zero step or an unknown name.
doc.MatchFields = True when the local minute, hour, day, month and weekday match the expression. Weekdays are 0 for Sunday through 6 for Saturday.
doc.MatchesLocal = True when a wall-clock instant in seconds, already shifted by the zone offset, matches the expression.
doc.OffsetSeconds = The offset of a time zone at an instant, in seconds; the fallback in minutes when the zone cannot be loaded.
doc.Next = The first moment (minute, or second for six fields) strictly after <code>after</code> that matches, in UTC seconds, or 0 when none is found within the scan limit.
doc.SetZone = Sets the zone name and fallback offset for the scheduler. The default is <code>UTC</code> with 0.
doc.Add = Registers a handler under a name and a schedule and returns the job id, or -1 when the schedule does not parse. A handler takes the job id as an <code>i64</code>.
doc.Count = The number of jobs that have not been removed.
doc.Name = The name of a job.
doc.IsBusy = True while the job is running from a firing; the scheduler skips a busy job.
doc.RunDue = Starts every job due at the given UTC time, each on its own thread, and returns how many started. A busy job is skipped.
doc.Run = Blocks and fires due jobs, checking several times a second, until <code>Stop</code> is called.
doc.RunBackground = Starts the same loop on its own thread and returns at once.
doc.Stop = Asks the scheduler loop to end after the current job. A job already running is not interrupted.
doc.Every = Runs a handler every n seconds, the first time n seconds from now. Returns the job id, or -1 for n of 0 or less.
doc.EveryBetween = Runs a handler after a random gap between two bounds in seconds, drawn again after each firing.
doc.Once = Runs a handler once at a UTC instant, then retires the job.
doc.DailyAt = Runs a handler every day at <code>"HH:MM"</code> or <code>"HH:MM:SS"</code> in the scheduler zone. Returns -1 when the time does not parse.
doc.WeeklyAt = Runs a handler on the given weekdays (for example <code>"mon-fri"</code>) at a clock time.
doc.MonthlyAt = Runs a handler on a day of the month (1 to 31) at a clock time.
doc.Total = Every id ever issued, including removed jobs. Use it to loop over ids.
doc.Find = The id of the first live job with that name, or -1.
doc.Remove = Retires a job; it never fires again and its id is not reused.
doc.SetTags = Replaces a job's tags with a comma-separated list.
doc.HasTag = True when the job carries the tag.
doc.RemoveByTag = Retires every live job with the tag and returns how many.
doc.Limit = Retires the job after n runs.
doc.StartAt = The job does not fire before this UTC instant.
doc.StopAt = The job does not fire after this UTC instant.
doc.FromCompletion = Counts an interval job's next gap from the end of a run, not its start.
doc.Queue = A firing that finds the job busy waits and runs after it, instead of being skipped.
doc.OnBefore = Registers a handler (taking the job id) that runs before every job.
doc.OnAfter = Registers a handler (taking the job id) that runs after every job.
doc.Runs = How many times the job has started.
doc.LastRun = When the last run started, in UTC seconds, or 0.
doc.LastFinished = When the last run finished, in UTC seconds, or 0.
doc.NextRun = The next firing after the given instant, honouring StartAt and StopAt, or 0 when there is none.
doc.RunNow = Starts a job immediately, outside its schedule. Returns false when it is busy.
doc.StopWait = Stops the loop, then waits up to a timeout in milliseconds for running jobs to finish.
