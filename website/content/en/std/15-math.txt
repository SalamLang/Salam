pkg.intro <<
<p>The <code>math</code> package holds what you would expect from a scientific calculator. Most of its functions work on <code>f64</code>; the ones whose names end in <code>I</code> or <code>I64</code> are the whole-number versions.</p>
<p>The angles in the trigonometric functions are in <strong>radians</strong>, not degrees. Use <code>Radians</code> and <code>Degrees</code> to convert.</p>
<p>For averages, medians and percentiles of a list use <a href="/std/stats/">stats</a>; for money and other exact decimals, <a href="/std/decimal/">decimal</a>; for whole numbers too large for <code>i64</code>, <a href="/std/bigint/">bigint</a>; for random numbers, <a href="/std/rand/">rand</a>.</p>
<h3>Behaviour worth knowing</h3>
<ul>
<li><code>Round</code> rounds halves up, toward positive infinity: <code>Round(2.5)</code> is 3 but <code>Round(-2.5)</code> is -2. Round the absolute value and put the sign back if you need halves away from zero.</li>
<li><code>Mod</code> keeps the sign of the first argument, as C's <code>fmod</code> does: <code>Mod(-7.0, 3.0)</code> is -1, not 2.</li>
<li><code>Factorial</code> returns an <code>i64</code> and is exact only up to 20; from 21 on the result overflows and is wrong.</li>
<li><code>Hypot</code> squares its arguments directly, so values above about 1e154 give infinity.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="guide-geometry">Distances, angles and circles</h2>
<p>Most geometry in a program is a distance or a direction between two points. <code>Hypot</code> gives the straight line distance, and <code>Atan2</code> gives the angle of a direction from all four quarters of the plane, which plain <code>Atan</code> cannot tell apart. Convert between degrees and radians at the edges of your program and keep radians inside it.</p>
{{example.math-guide-geometry}}
<p><code>fmt.FloatPrec</code> from <a href="/std/fmt/">fmt</a> prints a number with a fixed count of decimals, which keeps results such as <code>8.660254037844386</code> readable.</p>

<h2 id="guide-money">Growth, loans and rounding</h2>
<p><code>Pow</code> handles compound growth and <code>Log</code> answers the reverse question of how long it takes. To round to a number of decimal places, multiply by <code>Pow10</code>, round, and divide again. The last two lines show why: <code>f64</code> cannot store 0.1 exactly, so sums of prices pick up tiny errors.</p>
{{example.math-guide-money}}
<p>For real money, where every cent must add up, use <a href="/std/decimal/">decimal</a> instead of <code>f64</code>; the rounding trick above is fine for display and for estimates.</p>

<h2 id="guide-fractions">Whole numbers: Gcd and Lcm</h2>
<p>The greatest common divisor reduces fractions and finds the largest tile or package size that divides evenly; the least common multiple finds the next time two repeating events line up. Both work on <code>int</code>; <code>GcdI64</code> takes 64-bit values.</p>
{{example.math-guide-fractions}}

<h2 id="guide-stats">Mean and spread by hand</h2>
<p><code>Min</code>, <code>Max</code>, <code>Pow</code> and <code>Sqrt</code> are enough for a small summary of a list. For anything bigger, such as the median, percentiles or a correlation, the <a href="/std/stats/">stats</a> package already has the functions.</p>
{{example.math-guide-stats}}

<h2 id="guide-safe">NaN, infinity and comparing decimals</h2>
<p>Dividing by zero does not stop a Salam program when the numbers are <code>f64</code>: it gives infinity, or NaN for zero divided by zero, and both spread silently through later sums. Check results that come from user data with <code>IsNaN</code> and <code>IsInf</code>. Never compare two computed decimals with <code>==</code>; compare the size of their difference instead. <code>ClampI</code> and <code>ClampF</code> keep a value inside a range.</p>
{{example.math-guide-safe}}

doc.PI = Pi (π), the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.
doc.E = Euler's number (e), the base of the natural logarithm.
doc.TAU = Twice pi (τ); one full turn in radians.
doc.Sqrt = The square root of a number. For a negative number it gives NaN.
doc.Sin = The sine of an angle given in radians.
doc.Cos = The cosine of an angle given in radians.
doc.Tan = The tangent of an angle given in radians.
doc.Asin = The inverse sine, an angle in radians between minus π/2 and π/2.
doc.Acos = The inverse cosine, an angle in radians between zero and π.
doc.Atan = The inverse tangent, an angle in radians.
doc.Atan2 = The angle of the point (x, y) from the horizontal axis. Unlike <code>Atan</code> it works out the right quadrant. Note that <code>y</code> comes first.
doc.Log = The natural logarithm, to base e.
doc.Log10 = The logarithm to base ten.
doc.Log2 = The logarithm to base two.
doc.Exp = e raised to the power of the number.
doc.Floor = The largest whole number not greater than the value; for negatives it goes down (−2.5 gives −3).
doc.Ceil = The smallest whole number not less than the value.
doc.Round = Rounds to the nearest whole number, taking a half upwards, as JavaScript's <code>Math.round</code> does: 2.5 gives 3 and −2.5 gives −2. For money and exact rounding see the <a href="/std/decimal/">decimal</a> package.
doc.Abs = The absolute value of a decimal number.
doc.Pow = <code>base</code> raised to the power <code>power</code>.
doc.Cbrt = The cube root; it gives the correct negative answer for a negative number.
doc.Hypot = The hypotenuse of a right triangle, that is √(x² + y²). It squares the arguments directly, so inputs above about 1e154 overflow to infinity.
doc.Sign = The sign of a number: minus one, zero or one.
doc.Min = The smaller of two decimal numbers.
doc.Max = The larger of two decimal numbers.
doc.ClampF = Keeps a decimal number within the range <code>lo</code> to <code>hi</code>.
doc.MinI = The smaller of two whole numbers.
doc.MaxI = The larger of two whole numbers.
doc.AbsI = The absolute value of a whole number.
doc.ClampI = Keeps a whole number within the range <code>lo</code> to <code>hi</code>, for holding a percentage between zero and a hundred, for instance.
doc.Gcd = The greatest common divisor of two whole numbers.
doc.Lcm = The least common multiple of two whole numbers.
doc.AbsI64 = The absolute value of a 64-bit whole number.
doc.Mod = The remainder of dividing two decimal numbers; its sign follows the sign of <code>x</code>.
doc.Trunc = Drops the fractional part without rounding (−2.5 gives −2).
doc.IsNaN = True when the number is "not a number". NaN is not equal to itself, so <code>==</code> cannot detect it.
doc.Inf = Returns positive infinity.
doc.NaN = Returns a "not a number" value, the result of a meaningless operation such as zero divided by zero.
doc.IsInf = True when the number is positive or negative infinity.
doc.Sinh = The hyperbolic sine.
doc.Cosh = The hyperbolic cosine.
doc.Tanh = The hyperbolic tangent, also used in neural networks.
doc.Pow10 = Ten raised to the power <code>n</code>.
doc.Lerp = Linear interpolation: the point that lies between <code>a</code> and <code>b</code> at the fraction <code>t</code>. A <code>t</code> of zero is <code>a</code> and one is <code>b</code>.
doc.Radians = Converts degrees to radians.
doc.Degrees = Converts radians to degrees.
doc.Factorial = The factorial of <code>n</code>. Exact up to 20; from 21 the <code>i64</code> overflows and the result is wrong. For larger ones see the <a href="/std/bigint/">bigint</a> package.
doc.GcdI64 = The greatest common divisor of two 64-bit whole numbers.
doc.MinI64 = The smaller of two 64-bit whole numbers.
doc.MaxI64 = The larger of two 64-bit whole numbers.
