pkg.intro <<
<p>The <code>stats</code> package works on a <code>Vector&lt;f64&gt;</code> and never changes it. Variance and standard deviation come in two forms: the ordinary one for when the data is a <em>sample</em> of a larger population, dividing by n−1, and the "population" one for when you have the whole population, dividing by n.</p>

<h2 id="guide-report">A summary of a class</h2>
<p>The usual job is a short report on a list of numbers: how many, the middle, the spread and the extremes. Put the values in a <code>Vector&lt;f64&gt;</code> once and call the functions you need; none of them changes the vector. The standard deviation also lets you flag values that are far from the rest: a value more than one standard deviation from the mean is unusual in a small group.</p>
{{example.stats-guide-report}}
<p>A class or a survey is usually a sample, so <code>Stdev</code> is the right choice. Use <code>PopStdev</code> only when the list holds every member of the group you are describing, for example all the machines in a cluster.</p>

<h2 id="guide-latency">Response times: median and percentiles</h2>
<p>One slow request can pull the mean far away from what most users see. In this example nine requests take about 40 ms and one takes 1200 ms; the mean says 157 ms, while the median (the 50th percentile) says 41.5 ms. Monitoring therefore uses percentiles: p90 is the time 90% of requests stay under, and p99 shows the worst cases.</p>
{{example.stats-guide-latency}}
<p><code>Percentile</code> interpolates between the two nearest values, so with few samples the high percentiles land between a normal value and the outlier. Collect a few hundred values before you trust p99.</p>

<h2 id="guide-growth">Growth rates and the geometric mean</h2>
<p>When values are multiplied, as with yearly returns or growth factors, the arithmetic mean overstates the result. A gain of 50% followed by a loss of 40% is not an average gain. <code>Geomean</code> gives the single factor that, repeated, reaches the same end value.</p>
{{example.stats-guide-growth}}

<h2 id="guide-parse">Reading numbers from text</h2>
<p>Data usually arrives as text, with empty cells, labels such as <code>n/a</code> and sometimes Persian digits. Check each cell with <code>types.IsNumeric</code> from <a href="/std/types/">types</a>, turn Persian digits into English ones with <code>str.ToEnglishDigits</code> and parse with <code>conv.ParseFloat</code>. Count what you skipped, so that a report on bad data does not look like a report on good data.</p>
{{example.stats-guide-parse}}

<h2 id="guide-edge">Empty and tiny lists</h2>
<p>The functions do not fail on awkward input; they return a number, and that number can mislead. An empty vector gives 0 for the mean, the median and the maximum. One value has a variance of 0. A single zero makes the geometric mean 0, and a percentile above 100 is treated as 100. Check <code>len()</code> before you report anything.</p>
{{example.stats-guide-edge}}

doc.Sum = The sum of all the numbers.
doc.Mean = The arithmetic mean.
doc.Min = The smallest number.
doc.Max = The largest number.
doc.Median = The middle number after sorting, or the mean of the middle two when there is an even count. It stands up to outliers better than the mean.
doc.Variance = The sample variance, dividing by n−1.
doc.PopVariance = The population variance, dividing by n.
doc.Stdev = The sample standard deviation, the square root of the sample variance.
doc.PopStdev = The population standard deviation.
doc.Geomean = The geometric mean, which suits growth rates and ratios better than the arithmetic mean. Every number has to be positive.
doc.Percentile = The <code>p</code>th percentile, with p between zero and a hundred; the 90th percentile, for instance, is the value 90 per cent of the data falls below.
