pkg.intro <<
<p>This package computes where every box of a user interface goes, using the rules of CSS, so that a page drawn natively has the same geometry as the same page in a browser. You build a <code>Tree</code> of boxes, give each one a <code>Style</code> (display, size, margins, padding, border, flex properties and so on), and call <code>Compute</code> with the size of the window. <code>Layout</code> then gives each box its position, size, content box and scrollable area. The package draws nothing and knows nothing about fonts: text and images are leaves whose size comes from a measure callback you supply.</p>
<p>Block flow follows CSS 2 with margin collapsing between siblings, between a parent and its first or last child, and through empty boxes. Flexbox follows the CSS flexbox specification: flex base sizes, the grow and shrink loop with freezing, wrapping, every justify and align value, auto margins, baselines, reverse directions, <code>order</code> and right to left rows. Absolute and fixed boxes are placed against their containing block, including their static position when insets are auto. Floats, grid, inline text flow and writing modes other than horizontal are not done.</p>
<p>The algorithms are a port of <a href="https://github.com/DioxusLabs/taffy">Taffy</a>, and the package is tested against 911 of Taffy's fixtures measured in Chrome, in both box sizing models and both directions.</p>
<h3>Things to know</h3>
<ul>
<li>Lengths are already resolved: give pixels or percentages. Convert em, rem, vw and vh before building the style.</li>
<li>Defaults are the CSS initial values: <code>display</code> is block, <code>position</code> is static and <code>box_sizing</code> is content-box.</li>
<li>Ids are plain integers in the order of <code>AddNode</code>; the node you pass to <code>Compute</code> is the root.</li>
<li>Results are rounded to whole pixels the way Chrome rounds them. Turn this off with <code>SetRounding(false)</code> to read fractions.</li>
<li>Each node caches its last results. After a style change only the changed node and its ancestors are laid out again; call <code>MarkDirty</code> when the content behind a measure callback changes.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="guide-flex">A toolbar with flexbox and text</h2>
<p>A row with a growing title, a button and a badge moved first with <code>order</code>. The measure callback stands in for text: it answers how wide the text is with unlimited room, how narrow it can get, and how tall it is at a given width. Switching the direction to right to left mirrors the row.</p>
{{example.gui-cssbox-flex}}

<h2 id="guide-block">Block flow and absolute positioning</h2>
<p>The header's bottom margin and the card's top margin collapse into one 30 pixel gap, the card is centred with auto margins, and the close button is placed in the corner of the page, which is its containing block because it is positioned. A box with <code>display</code> none takes no space.</p>
{{example.gui-cssbox-block}}

<h2 id="guide-incremental">Changing one box</h2>
<p>The counters show the cache at work: a second <code>Compute</code> with nothing changed computes nothing, and changing one leaf recomputes that leaf and its ancestors. Laying out a 5,000 box page (50 wrapping flex rows of 100 text leaves) takes about 15 ms the first time on one desktop core, and about 0.7 ms after one leaf changes; most of that is rounding the whole tree.</p>
{{example.gui-cssbox-Tree}}
