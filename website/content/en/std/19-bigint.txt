pkg.intro <<
<p>An <code>i64</code> holds about nine quintillion. For anything larger, such as the factorial of 30 or a cryptographic key, use <code>bigint.Int</code>, whose size is limited only by memory.</p>
<p>Most functions return a new number and leave their arguments alone. The ones whose names say <code>To</code>, <code>From</code> or <code>By</code>, such as <code>AddTo</code>, change the number in place and are faster in a loop because they take no new memory. These numbers allocate; release the ones you no longer need with <code>Free</code>.</p>

<h2 id="guide-fibonacci">Past the end of i64</h2>
<p>The 93rd Fibonacci number is the first one that does not fit in an <code>i64</code>; with ordinary integers it would wrap around to a negative value without warning. With <code>bigint.Int</code> the loop just keeps going. <code>ToInt</code> converts back and tells you, through its <code>bool</code> argument, whether the value fitted, so you can move to plain integers when the numbers are small again.</p>
{{example.bigint-guide-fibonacci}}
<p>Each <code>Add</code> returns a new number, so the loop frees the value it no longer needs before it moves the names along. Without the <code>Free</code> calls the program would still be correct, but it would keep every intermediate number in memory.</p>

<h2 id="guide-inplace">Faster loops with the in-place functions</h2>
<p>In a long loop, a new number on every step costs an allocation each time. <code>MulIntBy</code>, <code>AddTo</code>, <code>ShlBy</code> and the other functions named with <code>To</code>, <code>From</code> or <code>By</code> change their first argument instead. Build the result in one variable and free it once at the end.</p>
{{example.bigint-guide-inplace}}

<h2 id="guide-digits">Digits of very large numbers</h2>
<p><code>ToStr</code> gives the decimal text, so its length is the number of digits. <code>DivModInt</code> divides by an ordinary integer and puts the remainder into an <code>i64</code>, which is the way to walk through the digits one by one, here to add them up. <code>TrailingZeros</code> counts zero bits, not decimal zeros.</p>
{{example.bigint-guide-digits}}

<h2 id="guide-parse">Reading and printing in other bases</h2>
<p><code>Parse</code> reads decimal text and reports through its <code>bool</code> argument whether the text was a whole number; an empty string or a stray letter fails. Persian digits are accepted. <code>ParseBase</code> and <code>ToStrBase</code> handle bases from 2 to 36, and <code>ToHex</code> is the usual form for keys and hashes. <code>ToIntClamped</code> gives the largest or smallest <code>i64</code> when the value is out of range.</p>
{{example.bigint-guide-parse}}

<h2 id="guide-rsa">Modular arithmetic: a toy RSA</h2>
<p>Public key cryptography is built on <code>ModExp</code>, <code>ModInverse</code> and <code>Gcd</code>. The program below makes an RSA key from two small primes, encrypts a number with the public exponent and decrypts it with the private one. <code>ModInverse</code> fails, through its <code>bool</code>, when the two numbers share a factor.</p>
{{example.bigint-guide-rsa}}
<p>This is for learning how the arithmetic works. Real RSA needs primes of more than a thousand bits, random padding and constant time code; for real keys and signatures use <a href="/std/crypto/">crypto</a>.</p>
